# Hepteract Distributor (`HSHepteractDistributor`, `hs-modules/hs-hepteracts/hs-hepteract-distributor.ts`)

The "Hepteract Distributor" setting (`enableHepteractDistributor`, QoL page, on by default). It spends the owned hepteracts on the hepteract types so that their balances follow ratios the player set.

## Panel
- **A "Distributor" button on the forge's welcome line opens the panel**, which then takes the place of the forge's three intro lines (`#hepteractWelcome`, `#hepteractExpository1/2`, hidden by a class on `#cubeTab7`, CSS only). A minimize button closes it. Open or closed is kept (`hepteractDistributorOpen`). No game element is moved: the button is absolutely placed, and the welcome line gets side padding so a long translation wraps instead of running under it.
- **The panel's title has a "?"** that shows the feature in a few lines on hover (`HELP_LINES`): update it when the feature changes. The mouse passes through its tooltip, so it closes as soon as the "?" is left. Each control keeps its own tooltip.
- **A warning line is always shown under the buttons** ("Distribute ascends several times in a row…"), chosen over a checkbox.
- **A run's status is also written to the Log tab** (`HSLogger.log`, one line, its lines joined by " | ": a log line shows no line break), and a stop as a warning, both through `escapeLogText`.
- **Not available** (Distribute disabled, the reason in the status line, checked when the panel opens, on hover and at each press): bookmark load (no live player), before the first singularity (no Auto craft), inside Exalt 3 (`limitedAscensions`) and Exalt 7 (`limitedTime`), and while autosing is active.
- **The Quark hepteract has no row** (it also spends quarks), as in quick expand.
- **A locked type** (its meter has no `/`) is left out of the run and of "Match current", and listed in the summary ("Locked: …"). It is not dimmed in the panel: built, then dropped. The meter's text is only right while the forge is drawn (it starts as "/" in the game's HTML), and types are only partly locked between a Challenge 15 exponent of 1e15 and 3.33e16.
- Chrome's "form field should have an id or name" advisory on the panel's fields is harmless (autofill only).

## Ratios and the plan
- **A ratio compares what the final balances are worth in hepteracts**: balance × hepteracts per unit (chronos 4, challenge 1: chronos's balance should be worth 4 times challenge's, so hold 20 times its units). 0 leaves the type alone. The singularity debuff is the same factor for every type. Expansions consume half of what each type crafted, the same doubling for all, so the spend follows the ratios closely too.
  - Not balances in units (the first version: "only the final amount owned matters"); changed after a player's report.
- **"Match current"** sets the ratios from capacity × hepteracts per unit. It gives repeating decimals since ratios are in hepteracts (Multiplier's 3e5 per unit): no fix wanted.
- **The rule is in `hs-hepteract-distributor-plan.ts`** (pure, no DOM): the type furthest behind its ratio (lowest balance × unit cost / ratio) gets its next step, "fill to the top", or "expand, then fill to the top" when its meter is full. Capacities only double, so a ratio that is not a power of two holds within one step.
- **When that type can't pay its step, the run stops** and the rest stays unspent: cheaper types would otherwise end far above their ratio (a multiplier unit costs 30 chronos units).
- **"Allow partly filled meters"** (off by default): what is left goes into that type anyway; it only expands when at least what the expansion consumes can be crafted back (the game's Auto rule), so a balance never goes down.
  - **A partial step ends the plan**, so it holds one at most: that type is crafted last and takes all that is left. What it can't use, under one of its units, stays for the next press. Not several partial steps: a type crafted before the others' money is spent would take its 5% margin out of it.
  - **An expansion paid back to the unit leaves the balance as it was**: the run counts a capacity change as crafted too.
  - **The status's "Next:" line gives the cost of a whole step**, also with this option on, even if the next press could still fill partly with what is left.
- **Overflux Orbs are a percentage, bought first** ("x % first"), not a ratio: they have no capacity.
- Not built yet: an optimizer that computes the ratios. The planner takes plain state and ratios, so an optimizer only has to produce ratios.

## Keep: a reserve never spent
`hepteractDistributorKeep`, first field of the row, nothing kept by default.
- **A power of ten of hepteracts, set aside before the Orbs**: the Orbs percentage and the ratios run on what is above it (`#spendable`, also the planner's stock). The Orbs percentage is scaled to the whole stock (`orbsPercent × spendable / owned`).
- **Why a power of ten, not a percentage**: bonuses count the hepteracts owned by powers of ten (Cx13 `1.03^log10`, the ambrosia Cube-Quark and Cube-Luck hybrid modules `floor(log10(x + 1))` digits).
- **The field takes the exponent only** ("Keep 1e [15]"). **The setting stores the distance to the stock, not the exponent** (a stored exponent goes stale as the stock grows): how many powers of ten below `floor(log10(owned))`, whole number, -1 = nothing kept. Typing converts it with the stock of that moment (an exponent above the stock's own gives distance 0, every digit); the field shows it back from the current stock, so the reserve follows the stock.
- **Empty = nothing kept**, with a greyed suggestion, `floor(log10(owned)) − 1` (at most a tenth of the stock, one digit less), a placeholder only.
- **Refreshed** (value and suggestion) when the panel opens, on the panel's `mouseenter` (value left alone while the field has the focus) and after a run, nothing else (the simplest).
- **The run fixes its reserve once**, right after the reward ascension (the reward counts in the reserve, not on top of it), never again during the crafts: it would fall as the crafts spend the stock it follows.
- **Float traps**: the amount is read as the literal `Number('1e' + n)` (`10 ** 23` is one float step above `1e23`), 300 at most (the game's cap). The game takes `floor(stock × percentage / 100)`, and on a large stock float rounding can take a few thousand more than asked: with a reserve the run spends `1e-12` of the stock less (`KEEP_SAFETY`), and stops if the stock ends under the reserve anyway.

## Crafting through ascensions
- **Crafting goes through the game's own Auto craft, so through ascensions**. A craft by hand also costs another resource (obtainium, offerings, cubes, tesseracts…), which can be what limits it; the game's Auto craft (`autoCraftHepteracts`, run by `reset('ascension')`, from singularity 1) only takes hepteracts. At an ascension, every type with Auto on gets `stock × hepteractAutoCraftPercentage / 100 / number of Auto types`, and spends all of it: fill, expand while the rest can pay back the expansion, partial fill at the end.
- **One ascension per type, with only that type's Auto on** and the percentage set to its exact share. The game's prompt only takes whole percentages, its formula any number: **`player.hepteractAutoCraftPercentage` is written directly with a fraction**, and put back at the end. It is the run's only direct write (chosen over whole percentages, which miss small steps on a large stock, and over a patcher global on the game's function).
- **The amount aims 5% above the planned cost** (`AMOUNT_MARGIN`): the auto-craft stops on a full meter as long as the rest can't pay back one more expansion, which takes at least a third more than the cost. So a cost slightly off still ends on full meters.
- **Everything else is clicked on the game's own switches**, read from the live player, and clicked back at the end (`GameSwitch`): the 8 hepteract Autos (Quark included) and Orbs Auto, the ascension confirmation (`#toggle31`), and the game's per-ascension automations (4 auto-opens, auto cube and platonic upgrades, tesseract building autobuy). They are plain flips with no dialog. Below singularity 10 the game ignores clicks on the auto-opens (and doesn't auto-open): not fatal.
- **The run, in order:** switches off; one ascension that collects the current ascension's reward, with the player's automations as they are (skipped without a Challenge 10 completion); automations suspended; Orbs, through Orbs Auto alone; the plan, on the stock now exact (the following ascensions have no Challenge 10 completion, so no reward); one ascension per type; everything put back.
- **With the ascension confirmation off, the Ascend click resets at once** (`resetCheck` then `resetConfirmation` have no `await` on that path), with no dialog: the whole run is one go, before the game ticks, paints or saves, so the player's switches and percentage are never seen changed. With it on, the game refuses to ascend until Challenge 10 is completed again.
- **Nothing to craft with what is owned: no ascension at all.** The reward of the current ascension is not known beforehand, so the player's ascension is left alone, and the status says to ascend first.
- **One save read per press**, for the singularity debuff on hepteract costs (`calculateSingularityDebuff('Hepteract Costs')`). It is then checked against the cost the game shows for a Chronos unit (a `mouseover` on `#chronosHepteract` fills `#hepteractCostText`, from the live state, at once): more than 1% apart, nothing is spent and nothing ascends.
- **Inside an ascension challenge (C11 to C15) it works the same**: the game restarts the same challenge at each ascension (tested).
- Seen in the game source only, not in game: on a phone, `#ascendbtn` opens a modal instead of ascending, and the run stops with "The game did not ascend".
- Dropped: crafting through the Expand and Max buttons inside `HSGameDialogs.run()`, skipping a type short of its other resource (a first version, never committed).

## Singularity reducers and Talisman Power
"Use singularity reducers and Talisman Power" (`hepteractDistributorUseReducers`, off by default, above the partly filled option): for the crafts only, the ambrosia tree is replaced by a temporary one, then the player's tree is loaded back (also on an error).
- **Upgrade levels are not in `player`** (they live in the game's private upgrade objects; `player.ambrosiaUpgrades` only holds what was invested). Only a loadout load, a purchase or the edit mode changes them. The live tree is read back from the invested amounts with the mod's cost formulas (`HSAmbrosia.#readLiveTree`), and given up when an amount is not the exact price of a level.
- **The temporary tree** (`HSAmbrosia.prepareSingReducerSwap`): the reducer that counts where the player is (reducer 1 outside an Exalt, with Hyperflux 4; reducer 2 inside), at the highest level the game's own validation accepts above the live one, and nothing else, except Talisman Power (`ambrosiaTalismanBonusRuneLevel`). That upgrade raises the Horseshoe rune (talisman rune bonus), which lowers every singularity penalty through `shopHorseShoe` (up to 30%): it is the tree's only other way into the hepteract cost.
- **Talisman Power fills the tree after the reducer** (it costs no blueberries and at most 1,000,000 ambrosia). The reducer level is chosen first, with Talisman Power at 0, so it never costs a reducer level; then Talisman Power goes to the highest level the validation accepts. Raised only where it makes hepteracts cheaper (Horseshoe shop upgrade owned, Horseshoe rune unlocked and below its cap of 300, upgrade not turned off by an Exalt); elsewhere it is kept at the live level. The gain is small: 100 levels are 2 rune levels with a maxed Horseshoe talisman, about 0.3% on the cost.
- **The cost is computed for the tree's Talisman Power level** (`calculateSingularityDebuff`'s third parameter, `getAmbrosiaUpgradeEffectsAtLevel`): the check against the game's shown cost only allows 1.1%, and that shown cost is already cut by up to 1%. Purple enchantment free levels only count once a level is bought, so the effect is not `levels / 200` from level 0.
- **"Also swap when only Talisman Power can be added"** (`hepteractDistributorTalismanAlone`, off by default, shown under the reducers option while that one is ticked, CSS only): with no reducer to add (already in the player's tree, locked, too expensive, or of no use at this singularity), the tree is still swapped to raise Talisman Power, at the price of the ambrosia bars' progress. Off: no swap then. Chosen over never swapping for it.
- **A tree is loaded through a slot** (`#loadTreeThroughSlot`): written into `player.blueberryLoadouts[n]` for the time of the click, then the slot's own loadout is put back. One synchronous step, so the swap stays inside the run's single go.
- **Which slot:** the one the game marks. When it marks none (after every singularity), the one the quickbar remembers, only if its loadout is exactly the live tree; else the run stops before anything, with a line asking to load or save a loadout. With a marked slot, unsaved changes to the tree are kept. At the end the live tree and every slot are as before, and the mark is where it was (or on the quickbar's slot).
- **The reward ascension keeps the player's tree**: the swap comes after it. The cost with the reducers is the debuff for the save's singularity count minus the reducers added, and is checked against the game's shown cost right after the swap.
- **Each load resets the ambrosia bars' progress**, as any loadout load does: accepted, not written back.
- **Reducers locked, not affordable, tree not readable, patch without `__HS_validateBlueberryTree`:** the run goes on without them, with a line in the status. No swap either where they bring nothing (singularity 50 and below, Antiquities rune).
- Ways not taken: a slot picked by the player (two slots to maintain, restore needs a mark), a heater loadout (needs a heat), the game's edit mode `#ambrosiaEditLevels` (a modal, and the mod would have to choose what to lower).

## Shared code and testing
- **`hs-hepteract-state.ts` is shared with `HSHepteracts`** (pure, the caller passes the live player): the game's constants per hepteract (`HEPTERACT_DATA`: base capacity, hepteracts per unit), the capacities (`hepteractCapacities`), the live read (`readHepteract`), one expansion (`expandHepteract`) and the units needed to hold a balance (`hepteractUnitsToReach`, used by the Platonic upgrade estimate). Quick expand's own estimate stays in `HSHepteracts`: it expands as soon as the balance reaches the base capacity, as the game's Expand button allows, where the Distributor only expands a full meter.
- **Testing headless:** `scripts/harness/hepteract-distributor.cjs <save>` presses Distribute on the real game; `scripts/tests/test-hepteract-plan.cjs` covers the plan, against a copy of the game's auto-craft.
- **Testing in game:** turn each hepteract's Auto off first, or the auto-craft refills and re-expands it at every ascension and overwrites `hsDev.set` values.
